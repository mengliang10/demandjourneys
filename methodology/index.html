Methodology

Businesses rarely have isolated marketing problems.

Demand, customers, channels, distribution, technology, data and measurement interact as one commercial system. Demand Journeys examines that system rather than optimizing individual channels in isolation.

The Commercial Flow

The Primary Commercial System

How demand travels from market origin to sustained margin optimization.

01 Market Economics

Demand

How demand is created, captured and evaluated.

Demand does not begin in an advertising campaign; it originates in genuine market needs, seasonal triggers, and macroeconomic conditions. Understanding demand requires isolating true commercial intent from fabricated traffic.

Core Questions:
  • Where is demand originating: organic market expansion, competitor vulnerability, or baseline brand equity?
  • What is the quality of current demand, and does it produce acceptable unit economics?
  • Are growth bottlenecks caused by insufficient demand generation or friction in downstream capture?
Demand Generation · Brand Demand · Paid Acquisition · Organic Discovery · Incremental Demand · Demand Quality · Customer Acquisition Economics
02 Search & Visibility

Discovery

How customers and machines find businesses.

Discovery is undergoing a fundamental structural transition. Buyers no longer discover services solely through ten blue search links; they use synthetic AI answers, conversational assistants, vertical search platforms, and marketplaces.

Core Questions:
  • How do traditional search engines, answer engines, and LLMs interpret your commercial entity?
  • Is technical architecture, structured data, and content discoverability prepared for AI crawler synthesis?
  • How reliant is discovery on paid intermediary placement versus durable organic authority?
SEO · GEO · AEO · AI Search · Search Platforms · Metasearch · Digital Marketplaces · Content Discoverability · Entity Understanding
03 Value & Segments

Customer

Who customers are and which customers create economic value.

Not all buyers create equal commercial margin. Effective commercial architecture identifies the highest-yield customer segments, their transaction economics, and the specific touchpoints where customer value is created or lost.

Core Questions:
  • Which customer segments generate profitable lifetime value versus high servicing costs?
  • Is customer data unified across touchpoints into an actionable single profile?
  • How does Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) inform Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) thresholds?
Segmentation · Customer Data · Customer Value · CRM · CDP · Customer Profiles · Lifecycle · LTV
04 Media & Channels

Acquisition

How businesses buy or generate customers.

Acquisition should be governed as an investment portfolio. We align paid media, performance marketing, social distribution, and programmatic buying to verifiable incrementality rather than platform-reported return figures.

Core Questions:
  • What percentage of paid acquisition is truly incremental versus bidding on existing demand?
  • What are the true media economics across paid search, programmatic, and social channels?
  • How do blended Marketing Efficiency Ratios (MER) and channel-level ROAS guide budget deployment?
Performance Marketing · Paid Media · Search · Social · Programmatic · CAC · ROAS · Incrementality · Media Economics
05 Friction & Experience

Conversion

How demand turns into transactions.

Driving qualified demand into a high-friction booking or purchasing experience destroys commercial value. Conversion focuses on eliminating cognitive, technical, and process barriers along the direct digital path.

Core Questions:
  • Where in the funnel are qualified buyers dropping out, and what friction causes abandonment?
  • Does the digital product, booking engine, or e-commerce checkout load fast and transparently?
  • How is personalization deployed to resolve specific buyer hesitancy rather than add decorative noise?
Customer Journey · UX · CRO · Digital Product · Booking Engines · E-Commerce · Checkout Friction · Personalization
06 Channel Governance

Distribution

Who controls the transaction and how much that costs.

Distribution determines operating margin. Particularly in hospitality and travel, surrendering customer ownership entirely to Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) and marketplaces creates severe margin erosion, while relying purely on direct channels can restrict volume.

Core Questions:
  • What is the total cost of distribution across direct booking, OTAs, wholesalers, and corporate intermediaries?
  • Where is margin leaking through unauthorized wholesaler discounting or rate parity violations?
  • What distinct value proposition compels the customer to transact directly?
Direct Channels · OTAs · Marketplaces · Metasearch · Platform Economics · Distribution Costs · Commission Economics · Digital Commerce
07 Loyalty & Lifetime

Retention

How customers become repeat customers.

Re-acquiring the same customer repeatedly resets customer acquisition costs on every transaction. Retention turns initial buyers into recurring commercial assets through structured lifecycle management and genuine utility.

Core Questions:
  • What triggers repeat purchase or rebooking behavior, and at what interval?
  • Does the loyalty program create genuine economic attachment or merely fund unredeemed liability?
  • How automated are lifecycle re-engagement journeys across email, messaging, and customer portals?
CRM · Loyalty · Lifecycle Marketing · Marketing Automation · Customer Engagement · Repeat Purchasing · Customer Value
08 Truth & Causality

Measurement

How businesses distinguish activity from causality.

Most marketing reporting tracks volume and correlation rather than business causality. Establishing a credible evidence base requires isolating incremental gain, reconciling analytics to financial ledgers, and understanding measurement limitations.

Core Questions:
  • What commercial evidence separates marketing causality from baseline organic momentum?
  • How do Multi-Touch Attribution (MTA) and Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM) frameworks balance top-down and bottom-up data?
  • Do marketing dashboards reconcile transparently with the general ledger and audited revenue?
Marketing Analytics · Attribution · MTA · MMM · Incrementality · Experimentation · Holdouts · Commercial KPIs · Customer Economics
09 Systems & Architecture

MarTech

How technology supports the commercial system.

Organizations frequently accumulate software licenses without achieving business integration. Technology exists to execute the commercial operating model, not to justify vendor subscriptions.

Core Questions:
  • How cleanly do CMS, CRM, CDP, booking engines, and analytics communicate?
  • Which tools are redundant, underutilized, or creating stranded maintenance overhead?
  • What governance structures ensure vendor contracts and enterprise RFPs deliver promised capabilities?
CMS · AEM · CRM · CDP · Analytics · Tagging · Marketing Automation · Data Flows · Customer Platforms · Architecture · Vendor Selection · Technology Governance
10 Operating System Shift

AI

How artificial intelligence changes commercial workflows.

Artificial intelligence is not treated here as marketing hype or speculative automation. It represents a fundamental shift in how consumers research and discover options, how teams synthesize intelligence, and how operational workflows are executed.

Core Questions:
  • How does conversational and agentic search alter customer discovery and comparison behavior?
  • Where can workflow automation reduce administrative friction in marketing operations?
  • How does machine learning improve predictive demand forecasting and revenue optimization?
Discovery · Research · Decision-Making · Customer Interaction · Marketing Operations · Content Production · Search · Measurement · Workflows · Organizational Capability
11 Continuous Loop

Optimization

How the entire system improves over time.

Optimization is not a one-off audit or temporary initiative; it is an executive operating discipline. It requires continuous experimentation, clear governance between internal teams and external agencies, and rigorous capital allocation.

Core Questions:
  • Is there a structured experimentation cadence with controlled testing and holdout groups?
  • Are agency incentives aligned with incremental commercial profit rather than gross media spend?
  • How routinely are commercial budgets reallocated based on proven performance evidence?
Experimentation · Testing · Operating Models · Agency Governance · Measurement · Budget Allocation · Technology Prioritization · Commercial Decision-Making
