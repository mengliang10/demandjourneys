# Demand Journeys — adversarial content review

Three independent agent reviews examined the local copy as a sceptical business owner, a hotel owner/GM, and a proof/offer editor. This is an editorial exercise, not research with actual prospective customers.

## Shared finding

The earlier copy made Meng Liang appear experienced and analytical, but required visitors to infer what to hire him for. It described the way he thinks before explaining the work he could do for their business.

## Objections and changes

| Buyer objection | Change made |
|---|---|
| “I understand the questions. What would he do?” | Replaced the opening question with “Win customers. Keep them. Grow profitably.” The introduction explicitly describes Meng Liang's help. A three-step panel explains investigation, recommendations and delivery support. |
| “Do I need to diagnose the business before contacting him?” | Added three visible situations: growth not delivering, activity without clear results, and technology without business benefit. Contact invites either a challenge or opportunity, with no finished brief or complete data required. |
| “What can I give him to work on?” | Each situation describes the symptom, what Meng Liang would do, and the decision it helps the buyer make. Service headings describe actions rather than categories. |
| “Is this just a report?” | Connected each of the four diagnostic outputs to a practical use. Clarified the distinction between initial assessment, focused advisory projects and leadership through delivery. |
| “What does this CV mean for my business?” | Added a “Useful when” explanation to each operator example. Specific work and relevant situations replace uninformative scale-only claims. |
| “Why is a hotel specialist teaching me basic booking economics?” | Replaced the hospitality ledger with practical changes he can help make and named Frasers experience. Four hotel situations describe work on channel costs, booking conversion, platforms and team delivery. |
| “Is this only for large groups?” | Explicitly allows starting with one property, one channel or one decision. |
| “Do I have to buy a workshop to find out if there is a fit?” | The homepage primary CTA and default enquiry type are now an introductory conversation. The diagnostic remains prominent as a scoped next step. |

## What remains bounded

- The diagnostic is a 90-minute session with four initial outputs: demand map, leakage points, measurement gaps and maturity scorecard. It does not include a detailed roadmap or implementation.
- Preparation is optional; discovery is possible when the problem is not yet defined.
- Fees remain private and are agreed before work begins. No free-workshop promise was introduced.
- Historical employers remain clearly identified as employers, not Demand Journeys clients.
- The +56% figure remains website room-night growth in 2023, not revenue, profitability or measured incremental demand.
- The removed Accor agency-fee figures and public recommendation-letter references remain absent.
- No new historical metrics, guaranteed gains or client results were invented.

## Second review

All three agents reviewed the revised files. They found no remaining material buyer-comprehension, evidence or offer-scope blocker. Their final corrections were incorporated: welcome growth opportunities as well as problems, replace “Brand.com” with “your website”, replace accidental instructions to the buyer with statements of what Meng Liang does, and label room-night growth precisely.
